What the report shows

It adds up the differences of closed inventories per product. If the same deviation repeats itself, a goods movement between two sites is usually missing. A one-off outlier was more likely a miscount. Grouping is by product family, totalled over the product, because the article number differs from site to site.

The product families start collapsed. The sum of the differences is already in the header. A click on the family shows its subfamilies and its products, “Expand all” opens all of them at once. A family above others totals everything below it, in its own unit.

Under Product families the report can be restricted to single families, for the PDF as well. Subfamilies are indented. A tick on the family above sets or clears all of them below, and afterwards each one can be ticked or unticked on its own. Exactly what is ticked counts. The tick “Only products counted at more than one site” applies on top: within the chosen families only what has a result at more than one site remains.

Under PDF title the sheet gets a heading of its own, such as “Drinks in summer”. It stands at the top of the PDF, and the file is named after it. If the field stays empty, the sheet is called “Differences across inventories”. The page itself keeps its heading.

Every difference carries its sign. +12 means there was more than expected. -12 means something was missing. The colour says the same: green means it matched. Yellow means there was more. Red means something was missing. Both go for the sums as well, only there the colour is quieter. A sum is an overview and not a finding. How much colour you see is in your account menu under “Colours of the difference”.

If a product had neither a target stock nor a difference by hand in any of the inventories, the difference reads “-”: there was nothing to compare. If both were missing in only some of them, only the others count, and below it says how many inventories the sum comes from.
