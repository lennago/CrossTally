What splitting is for

The opposite direction of merging. Sometimes it turns out that one product is really two: a site carries something else under the same name, or two products were merged by mistake. The articles concerned then move to a new product. A click on a row pushes it to the other side.

What the new product inherits

Everything that makes up the product: base unit, product family and the weighing details (containers, grams per unit). The moved articles keep their inventory type. At first the two are the same product. Only afterwards is the new one changed into what it really is.

The packs

Packs belong to the product. The new one therefore gets a copy of every pack, including the inactive ones. Location defaults and counted quantities of the moved articles then point to the copy, with their pack and quantity. So nothing is lost. Whatever the new product does not carry can be set inactive on its page.

What stays where it was

The recipes. They point at the product and not at a site, and a recipe cannot be cut in half. If the product sits in one, check afterwards whether the prepared item can still be resolved at the sites of the new product. Otherwise an ingredient is missing there. The message after splitting points this out.
