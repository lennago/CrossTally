Format

First choose the system the file comes from. It determines which file is expected and how it is read. For WinTICKET that is the site's article stock list, as xlsx, xls or csv. The help next to the file field names the right file for each format.

In WinTICKET (its menus are German) the list sits under Berichte → Warenwirtschaft → Artikelbestand → Artikelbestandsliste nach Artikelbezeichnung (708). When saving, choose “Excel-Datei ohne Formatierung” (Excel file without formatting).

What the plan contains

Only what needs a decision. The import recognises an article by number and name: whatever the site already carries exactly like this, and about which the file says nothing different, is missing from the plan, as is everything not countable. How many those were is stated in the message above the plan.

Every block is structured by article group, and both levels can be expanded and collapsed. The head of an article group carries buttons that set all of its lines at once. A whole article group is thus done with one click, deposits and vouchers as Not countable, for instance.

A plan has at most {{ hoechstens_planzeilen }} lines, because applying it sends it back to the server in one piece. If it would grow larger, the import says so when the file is read. Then spread the article groups over several exports and import them one after the other. An article group missing from a file stays untouched (see Missing from the file).

New articles

Active means create and carry it, Inactive means create it but do not count it for now, such as seasonal goods. Discontinued creates the article for the record only: it takes neither number nor product and gets no line in any inventory. In the counting app it can be added at the very end if the inventory counts its inventory type. That is what is suggested when the file holds no quantity per pack. Not countable puts the product into the family “Not countable”: for deposits, vouchers and everything the merchandise management system carries as an article without it being one. That applies at every site. One click moves the line into the matching block. An article cannot be left uncreated. It would otherwise show up here at every import again.

The column Inventory type says in which tab the new article is counted. As for the product takes the type the other articles of the product already stand in. A new product thus goes into the first type of the list. Another choice applies to this article only.

Discontinued

This article existed before and is discontinued. Bring back makes it active again. If another article carries the number in the meantime, that one is discontinued in the process. The note under the line names it.

Conflicts

The article is carried, but the file says something else: a different article group, a different unit or a quantity per pack that is not kept on the product. Nothing is preselected: every conflict is decided separately, and as long as one is open the plan cannot be applied. With the choice the line moves to the resolved conflicts. The choice can still be changed there. Leave touches nothing. The line then shows up here again at the next import. Change takes the article group and the unit from the file, together with the selected product and the packs in the field.

The unit is the one of the site. The product keeps its own, because that is the unit the report across sites uses. If the site counts in a different dimension than the product (pieces instead of kilograms), the line asks for the conversion: how many units of the product one piece is. The field names the direction (“1 pcs = how many g?”): a pack of 1 kg of a product carried in g is 1000, not 0.001. Without it the line is not applied. The packs in the field are in the unit of the file and are converted into the unit of the product. If the plan could not show the product's packs in this unit because the conversion was still missing, the note names them. They stay as they are, with their names and state, and the field only adds what the product lacks. Whoever enters the conversion gets the line back with the product's packs before it is applied.

A new unit waits as long as an inventory that is not closed yet carries a target, an open target stock line or a difference by hand for the article. Those numbers are in the current unit. Closed results keep their unit anyway.

If an article differs in several respects, Change offers which of them is applied: article group, unit, packs. Everything is selected at first. Whatever is deselected stays as it is and shows up here again at the next import. If the unit stays, the article keeps its conversion too, and the line does not ask for it.

Number with a different name

Whether it was renamed or reassigned is not something the file reveals. Rename carries the existing article on under the new name. Replace discontinues it and creates a new one beside it. As what, is offered below. If there is no product with the new name, the one of the existing article is preselected. A reassigned number mostly belongs to another product, so check the product before Replace.

Missing from the file

These articles are carried by the site from the same system, the file no longer knows them. WinTICKET exports every selected article group in full, so a missing number has been delisted there. The choices are Leave, Inactive and Discontinued. Leave is preselected: an accidentally incomplete export must not clear anything away.

An article group for which the file holds not a single line counts as not selected. Its articles do not enter the plan at all. Anyone who knows that nothing changed in an article group simply leaves it out of the export. Which ones those were is stated in the message above the plan. If more than half of a selected article group is missing, a warning appears: the export was probably filtered.

Product

The product is the identity of an article across all sites. Left empty, a new one is created, either under the name of the article or under the name you enter below. A site carries the same product only once. If two lines reach for the same one, both are marked red. If a new article carries the name of a product that the site already carries under a different number, nothing is preselected: the new product then needs a name of its own.

Packs

The pack field holds everything the product carries, plus the quantities that only appear in the file. Whatever is already right stays untouched when the plan is applied. Each quantity occurs only once per product.

The field belongs to the product the plan suggested. If you choose another one, or a product where none was suggested, the line comes back with that product's packs on Apply now. Check them. Only the next apply writes. The same if you create a new product instead of the suggested one: then the field holds only the packs from the file. And if you change the conversion the product's packs in the field were converted with. If the article carries one already, it is in the field.

Packs are matched by quantity, not by name. Changing a name renames the pack, and the lines already counted stay attached to it. A new quantity creates a pack. A deleted line sets the pack to inactive. Nothing is deleted, because counts from earlier inventories hang on a pack.

Packs belong to the product and apply at every site. What is dropped here is dropped everywhere.

If the file names the quantity of an inactive pack, it stands in the field under its own name, and the note under the line says so. Applying then makes it active again, at every site, and the message afterwards counts it. Whoever does not want that takes the line out of the field. If the site already carries the article, the line is a conflict, and Change brings the pack back.

If the name already exists on the product with a different quantity, a name of its own is suggested (“crate 20”). That way the existing pack stays as it is and the site gets a second one. An inactive pack keeps its name too. If the suggestion is taken as well, it counts on (“crate 20 (2)”). The name can be changed.

For products counted in pieces, Einzeln = 1 (the loose single unit, stored under its German name) is in the field as well. The merchandise management system only knows the purchase pack, but what stands on the shelf is an opened one. If the product carries the single unit inactive, it stays that way.
