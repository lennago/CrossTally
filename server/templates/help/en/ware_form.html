What hangs on the product

Base unit and packs apply to all sites. Number, name and article group per site, on the other hand, sit on the article.

Inventory type

The type belongs to the product's articles. Here it can be set for all of them at once, at every site. Without a choice each stays as it is. If the articles are in different types, nothing is preselected. A new product has no articles yet and therefore no field for it: its first article gets its type in its own form.

Changing the unit

The base unit can be changed later as well. What is held in it is converted in the process: the quantities of the packs and the quantities in recipes. Shares in percent stay as they are. A box of 40 pieces of 125 g each becomes one of 5 kg. The product's conversions follow along. Target stocks and the results of earlier inventories stay as they are: they are in the site's unit, and every site gets its conversion to the product anew. Counted packs stay too: two boxes are still two boxes.

From g to kg or from litres to millilitres the application converts by itself. When switching between pieces, weight and volume it needs the conversion: how many of the new unit make one of the previous? If a piece weighs 125 g and the product will be carried in kg, 0.125 belongs there. If the product already carries the conversion, say “125 g per pcs”, the field can stay empty. The application then converts with it. Packs submitted along with it still count in the previous unit. If a pack would become too small or too large in the process, or if two would land on the same quantity, everything stays as it was. The same goes for a quantity in a recipe, a conversion of the product or the conversion of a site that would grow too large. The page names what does not fit. The loose single unit exists only in pieces: in kg or litres it becomes an ordinary pack “Einzeln” with its quantity, and back in pieces the single unit again.

Conversions

What one unit of the product is in the other dimensions. The direction is always the same: mass and volume per piece (“g per pcs”, “ml per pcs”, for a product in kg “kg per pcs”), mass per volume (“g per l”, that is the density: water 1,000, syrup about 1,300) and everything per portion (“pcs per portion”, “g per portion”). Within its own dimension the unit converts by itself, there is no field for that. An entry the form shows the other way round (“kg per pcs”) comes back with six significant digits. Whatever goes beyond that is lost on the next save.

The entries are needed in two places. The scale delivers grams and needs the weight of a piece or a litre as soon as the product is not carried in g or kg. And a product family totals in its lead unit: a bottle in a family that counts in litres needs “ml per pcs”, a bag of nuts in a family that counts in portions needs “g per portion”. What no family and no scale needs stays empty. When the unit changes, the application converts the conversions along.

Weighing

Is weighed means: the product goes loose onto the scale, and when counting an entry of its own appears for it beside the packs: gross weight in grams, and the tare of the chosen container is deducted. That works in every unit. If the product is not carried in g or kg, the scale needs the “g per …” from above. The containers here are the usual ones. “No container” (scale tared) is always offered when counting anyway and needs no tick.

Packs

Counting is quantity × amount: a crate of 20 bottles gets the amount 20, the single bottle the amount 1. Each amount may occur only once per product: “crate” and “case” of 20 each could not be told apart while counting.

A pack a running inventory already counts on can only be deleted once that inventory is closed. Until then it can be set inactive on the product's page.
