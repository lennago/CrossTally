What merging is for

If the same product is named differently at two sites, the import creates two products. Here they become one again: articles, packs and recipe lines of the dissolved product move over to the one that remains, and the dissolved one disappears afterwards.

Which product remains

That can be chosen, no matter whose page you merge from. Name, inventory type, product family and the weighing details (containers, grams per unit) come from the one that remains. The inventory type only as long as its articles agree on one. Otherwise every moving article keeps its own, and the product then stands as mixed. So the better maintained one should remain. Whatever the other had of its own in these details is lost.

The preview first, then the button

“Preview” shows what would happen: which packs merge or move, and which sites carry both. Nothing is changed until then. Writing happens only with “Merge for good”, after a confirmation. Whoever changes the choice in the preview gets the preview for it first. Merging cannot be undone.

What does not work, and when you are asked

If the two are carried in different units, the dissolved one is converted into the unit of the one that remains: its pack quantities, the quantities in its recipe lines and the conversion of every site. Shares in percent stay as they are. Between g and kg or litres and millilitres this happens by itself. Between pieces, weight and volume the field Conversion asks for it: how many units of the remaining one is one unit of the dissolved one? The sites keep counting in their own unit afterwards. If a piece good is absorbed into kg or litres, its loose single unit becomes an ordinary pack there, because the single unit exists only in pieces. If a pack would become too small or too large in the process, or a quantity in a recipe or the conversion of a site too large, nothing is merged. The field Conversion names what does not fit.

Two obstacles cannot be cleared here and are reported: when both sit in the same recipe, and when only one of the two belongs to “Not countable”. A third one depends on which one remains: if the dissolved one sits in a recipe with shares in percent, the one that remains has to know what one unit weighs. That is entered on its page first, or the other one remains. Identical pack names, by contrast, are the normal case, because it is the same product. You are only asked where the same name means a different quantity or where the same quantity runs under two names.

And where an active pack of the dissolved product would be absorbed into one that the remaining product carries only inactive. The preview highlights it and asks: Activate again brings it back, at every site, because the pack belongs to the product. Leave inactive hangs the defaults of the dissolved product on it, and there they rest. New inventories get no row for an inactive pack.

A site carries both

Then the same thing exists there twice, under two numbers, active or inactive. The article of the dissolved product is discontinued there and counts as the one that remains from then on. If the merchandise management system keeps a stock on its number, that stock is added to the one that remains when the target stock is imported. The article import no longer offers the number for bringing back. Nothing changes about the remaining article, not even when it is inactive. Which one remains follows from the choice above of which product remains. If one of the two is already discontinued, there is no conflict, and both stay as they are.

Every expectation of the discontinued article has a tick: take over or let it go with discontinuing. Taken over at a location where the one that stays is not expected yet, the expectation moves with its position and packs. Where it is already expected, the packs are added and the expected quantities of the same pack are summed. Lines in a running inventory stay as they are: counted is counted.
